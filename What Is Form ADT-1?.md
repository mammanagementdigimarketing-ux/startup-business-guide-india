# What Is Form ADT-1? When Must a Company Inform the ROC About Auditor Appointment?

Form ADT-1 is the prescribed notice used by a company to inform the Registrar of Companies (ROC) about the appointment of its statutory auditor. For an auditor appointed or re-appointed by members under Section 139(1) of the Companies Act, 2013, the company must file the notice with the ROC within 15 days of the meeting at which the appointment is made. This filing records the auditor’s details and the appointment period in the Ministry of Corporate Affairs (MCA) system.

# What Is Form ADT-1 Under the Companies Act, 2013?

Form ADT-1 is titled “Notice to the Registrar by company for appointment of auditor”. It is governed by Section 139 of the Companies Act, 2013 and Rule 4(2) of the Companies (Audit and Auditors) Rules, 2014. The form captures company information, the nature and date of appointment, auditor or audit-firm particulars, the period of appointment and other statutory declarations.

The legal basis appears in [Section 139 of the Companies Act, 2013](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/2114/5/A2013-18.pdf?v=20260519200129) and [Rule 4(2) of the Companies (Audit and Auditors) Rules, 2014](https://www.mca.gov.in/Ministry/pdf/NCARules_Chapter10.pdf). The form is an ROC intimation; it does not itself appoint the auditor. The appointment must first be validly approved through the process required by the Act.

# When Must a Company File ADT-1?

## Appointment or re-appointment at the AGM

Under Section 139(1), members appoint an auditor at the company’s first annual general meeting (AGM), with the auditor generally holding office until the conclusion of the sixth AGM and thereafter subject to the statutory appointment cycle. The company must inform the auditor and file notice of the appointment with the ROC within 15 days of the meeting. The explanation to Section 139(1) makes clear that appointment includes re-appointment.

For example, if a private limited company appoints or re-appoints its statutory auditor at an AGM held on 20 September, the ADT-1 filing deadline is calculated as 15 days from that meeting date. The company should not wait until its annual financial statements are ready or until the next annual compliance filing.

## First auditor of a newly incorporated company

The first auditor follows a separate appointment route under Section 139(6) for a company other than a Government company. The Board must appoint the first auditor within 30 days from the date of registration. If the Board does not do so, it must inform the members, who must appoint the auditor at an extraordinary general meeting within 90 days. The first auditor holds office until the conclusion of the first AGM.

The statutory filing position for a first auditor should be handled carefully. Rule 4(2) expressly refers to the notice required under the fourth proviso to Section 139(1), while the revised ADT-1 notified in 2025 includes a selectable category for “First auditor by Board of directors/members/C&AG”. This creates a distinction between the express wording of the rule and the expanded options in the form. Companies should check the latest MCA FAQs and portal instructions; filing ADT-1 for the first auditor is a prudent record-keeping step where the portal permits it, but the statutory appointment deadline under Section 139(6) remains separate.

The revised form was notified through the [Companies (Audit and Auditors) Amendment Rules, 2025](https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2025/263595.pdf), effective from 14 July 2025.

# What Information and Documents Are Needed?

The company should prepare the information required by the current MCA form and retain the underlying appointment records. Depending on the appointment category and the portal’s current requirements, the following are commonly relevant:

- Company Corporate Identity Number (CIN), legal name, registered-office address and company email ID.
- Board or members’ resolution and the relevant meeting date, as applicable.
- Auditor’s written consent to act and certificate confirming eligibility and non-disqualification under Section 141.
- Auditor or audit-firm details, including name, address, PAN and membership or firm registration particulars, as applicable.
- Appointment tenure, financial year details and information about previous appointment or rotation, where relevant.
- Digital Signature Certificate (DSC) of the authorised signatory and any professional certification required by the form.

The company should verify that the auditor’s consent, eligibility certificate, resolution and details entered in ADT-1 are consistent. Incomplete or mismatched information can lead to resubmission, rejection or delays in completing the ROC record.

# How to File Form ADT-1 on the MCA Portal

1. Complete the auditor appointment process under the relevant provision of the Companies Act and obtain the auditor’s written consent and eligibility certificate.
2. Log in to the MCA portal and locate the ADT-1 e-form under company e-filing or compliance services.
3. Enter the company’s CIN and confirm the company particulars displayed by the portal.
4. Select the correct nature of appointment and enter the meeting date, appointment date, auditor details and tenure.
5. Attach the documents required by the current form and instruction kit.
6. Validate the form, affix the required digital signatures and submit it with the applicable professional certification, if required.
7. Pay the prescribed filing fee and save the Service Request Number (SRN), challan and filed-form acknowledgement.

After submission, the company should check the filing status on the MCA portal and retain the acknowledgement with its statutory records. The SRN and filed copy may also be useful when completing related annual filings.

# ADT-1 Filing Fees and Late Filing Consequences

The normal filing fee is determined under the applicable Companies (Registration Offices and Fees) Rules and the MCA fee schedule, including the company’s relevant capital category where applicable. Additional fees may apply when a form is filed after its due date. Because fee schedules and portal calculations can change, companies should use the fee displayed by the MCA system at the time of filing rather than relying on an old online fee chart.

Failure to comply with the auditor-appointment provisions can also attract consequences under Section 147 of the Companies Act, 2013. The statutory penalty depends on the contravention and the persons responsible; a delayed ADT-1 should therefore not be treated as merely an administrative inconvenience. Companies should review the applicable provision and current law before calculating exposure or taking corrective action.

The penalty framework is set out in [Section 147 of the Companies Act, 2013](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/2114/5/A2013-18.pdf?v=20260605114304).

# Common Mistakes Companies Should Avoid

- Calculating the 15-day deadline from the date the form is prepared instead of the date of the appointment meeting.
- Entering an incorrect appointment category, auditor name, membership number or firm registration number.
- Failing to obtain the auditor’s written consent and eligibility certificate before appointment.
- Overlooking auditor rotation and tenure restrictions where Section 139(2) applies.
- Assuming the first-auditor appointment deadline and the ADT-1 filing question are identical.
- Not saving the SRN, payment receipt and final filed form for the company’s records.

# Keeping Auditor Appointment Compliance on Track

Companies can manage auditor appointment filing more reliably by maintaining an annual compliance calendar, recording AGM and Board meeting dates, tracking auditor tenure and preparing appointment documents before the relevant meeting. A simple internal checklist shared between directors, finance teams and the company’s compliance professional can help prevent missed deadlines and inconsistent filings.
