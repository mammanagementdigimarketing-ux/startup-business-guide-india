# What Records Should Employers Maintain Under the Karnataka Shops and Commercial Establishments Act?

**Employers covered by the Karnataka Shops and Commercial Establishments Act, 1961 must maintain accurate employee and establishment records, including appointment orders, attendance and wage details, leave information, and prescribed notices and returns.** The records help demonstrate compliance with working-hour, weekly-holiday, wage and leave requirements. The exact obligations depend on the establishment’s coverage and any applicable exemptions or overlapping labour-law requirements.

## First, Check Whether the Karnataka Shops Act Applies

The Karnataka Shops and Commercial Establishments Act, 1961 applies to covered shops and commercial establishments in the state. However, employers should check the Karnataka Shops and Commercial Establishments (Amendment) Act, 2026, which came into force on 4 September 2026. It added an exemption for an establishment employing ten or more workers if it is already registered under the Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, 2020. It also provides that a godown or storage facility within 100 metres of the principal establishment does not require separate registration in the specified circumstances. [Karnataka Act No. 38 of 2026 – Gazette notification](https://vishaalconsultancy.com/Gazette_notification.pdf).

The four central Labour Codes commenced on 21 November 2025, while Karnataka’s corresponding state rules have been reported as drafts in the latest available regulatory tracker. Employers should therefore review both the applicable Code requirements and the saved state rules, and confirm the latest position before relying on an exemption. The discussion below focuses on the Karnataka Act and Rules where they continue to apply.

## 1. Appointment Orders and Employee Details

Section 6A of the Karnataka Shops Act requires an employer to issue a written appointment order within 30 days of an employee’s appointment. The order must state the employee’s name, designation, wage scale and terms and conditions of employment. Rule 24(9-A) of the Karnataka Shops and Commercial Establishments Rules, 1963 prescribes Form Q for the appointment order. [Karnataka Shops Act, 1961](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/7609/1/8_of_1962_%28e%29.pdf) and [Karnataka Shops and Commercial Establishments Rules, 1963](https://www.datocms-assets.com/40521/1630504261-karnataka-shops-and-commercial-establishments-rules-1963.pdf) provide the statutory framework.

Maintain a copy of each appointment order and supporting employee particulars, such as date of joining, designation, department and wage details. Keep employee records updated when there are changes to role, pay or employment status. The 2026 amendment also introduced a requirement to issue a service certificate within seven days after receiving an employee’s application and prohibits employers from retaining employees’ original educational, experience or other original documents.

## 2. Attendance and Wage Records: Combined Form T

Rule 24(9-B) requires employers to maintain a combined Muster Roll-cum-Register of Wages in Form T for employees. The prescribed form captures employee identity and employment particulars, date of joining, designation or department, wage details, daily attendance, payable days and overtime hours. It also contains wage and deduction information, payment mode and employee acknowledgement. [Form T in the Karnataka Rules](https://www.datocms-assets.com/40521/1630504261-karnataka-shops-and-commercial-establishments-rules-1963.pdf).

Where Form T is maintained, the Rules state that a separate muster roll or wage register containing the same information need not be maintained. Employers should nevertheless ensure that all required fields are completed accurately and that payroll entries reconcile with bank transfers, payslips and accounting records. Separate records may still be necessary under other laws or for information not covered by Form T.

## 3. Leave With Wages Register and Employee Leave Book

Rule 8 requires a Leave with Wages Register in Form F. It records service and leave particulars, including days worked, leave earned, leave taken and leave balance. The register must be preserved for three years after the last entry and produced to an Inspector on demand. Rule 9 also requires the employer to provide each employee with a Leave with Wages Book in Form H, which belongs to the employee. [Rules 8 and 9 – leave records](https://www.datocms-assets.com/40521/1630504261-karnataka-shops-and-commercial-establishments-rules-1963.pdf).

A practical leave-management system should record applications, approvals, dates of absence, leave balances and wages paid for leave. These records help the employer verify entitlements and respond to employee queries.

## 4. Overtime, Working Hours and Weekly Holiday Records

The Act regulates daily and weekly working hours, rest intervals, spread-over, overtime and weekly holidays. Form T includes a field for total overtime hours worked, so employers should record overtime on the relevant day and retain supporting shift rosters or approval records. Payroll records should show the corresponding overtime payment where payable.

Rule 24(4) requires employers to display a notice in Form P specifying the weekly holiday or holidays given to employees. The notice must be displayed before the employees concerned cease work on the Saturday immediately preceding the first week to which it applies. The Rules also require prescribed extracts of the Act and Rules to be displayed in Kannada and in the language of the majority of employees. [Rule 24 – notices and records](https://www.datocms-assets.com/40521/1630504261-karnataka-shops-and-commercial-establishments-rules-1963.pdf).

## 5. Combined Annual Return in Form U

Under Rule 24(9-C), employers must submit a combined Annual Return in Form U to the concerned Inspector on or before 31 January every year. Form U collects establishment and employer details, employment numbers, days and hours worked, wages paid, employee exits, leave information, maternity benefits, deductions and contract-labour particulars. Where Form U is furnished, the Rules dispense with other annual returns containing the same information. [Form U – Combined Annual Return](https://www.datocms-assets.com/40521/1630504261-karnataka-shops-and-commercial-establishments-rules-1963.pdf).

## 6. Visit Book and Record Retention

Rule 24(11) requires an employer to maintain a Visit Book so an Inspector can record observations or defects identified during an inspection. The employer must produce it whenever required. The Rules also provide that entries relating to a day must be made on that day. Unless a different retention period is prescribed, records and notices relating to a calendar year must be preserved until the end of the next calendar year. The longer three-year preservation period specifically applies to the Form F leave register.

## A Practical Record-Keeping Checklist for Employers

- Keep the establishment registration details and applicable exemption or OSH Code registration documents current.
- Issue and retain Form Q appointment orders and update employee particulars.
- Maintain Form T with daily attendance, wages, payable days and overtime information.
- Maintain Form F and provide employees with their Form H leave books.
- Display the Form P weekly-holiday notice and required statutory extracts.
- File Form U by 31 January each year, where the requirement applies.
- Keep the Visit Book available and preserve records for the applicable period.

## Building a Consistent Compliance Process

A reliable system does more than keep registers ready for inspection. HR, payroll and business administrators should use consistent employee identifiers, reconcile attendance with wage payments, update leave balances promptly and retain digital backups of supporting documents. Employers operating multiple branches or separate work locations should check whether records and notices must be maintained at each relevant premises.
