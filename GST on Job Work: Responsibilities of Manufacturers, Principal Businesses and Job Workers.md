# GST on Job Work: Responsibilities of Manufacturers, Principal Businesses and Job Workers

Job work is common in Indian manufacturing, where a business sends raw materials, components or capital goods to another person for processing, finishing, assembling, testing or a similar activity. Under GST, a registered principal can generally send eligible goods for job work without paying tax at the time of movement, provided the prescribed documentation and time limits are followed. The principal remains responsible for important records and input tax credit (ITC), while the job worker must correctly account for the processing service and return or transfer the goods as instructed.

## What Qualifies as Job Work Under GST?

Section 2(68) of the Central Goods and Services Tax (CGST) Act, 2017 defines job work as treatment or processing undertaken by a person on goods belonging to another registered person. The registered person whose goods are processed is called the principal for the job-work provisions. The definition matters: a contract described commercially as outsourcing or manufacturing may not automatically qualify for the special job-work procedure if the statutory conditions are not met. See [Section 2(68) and Section 143 of the CGST Act](https://taxinformation.cbic.gov.in/content-page/explore-act/1000420/1000001/Job%20work/ACTS) for the legal framework.

## 1. Responsibilities of the Principal Manufacturer

### Issue a proper job-work challan

The principal must send inputs, semi-finished goods or capital goods under a challan in accordance with Rule 45 read with Rule 55 of the CGST Rules. A challan is required even when a supplier dispatches goods directly to the job worker on the principal’s instructions. It should identify the goods and quantities clearly and contain the prescribed particulars. Keep the challan reference linked to dispatch, receipt, processing and return records.

### Track statutory return or supply periods

Under Section 143, inputs sent for job work must generally be brought back or supplied from the job worker’s place of business within one year. For capital goods, the general period is three years. Moulds and dies, jigs and fixtures, and tools are excluded from the capital-goods return time limit specified in this provision. The principal should track each dispatch date and quantity, including movements through subsequent job workers.

If the goods are not returned or supplied within the applicable period, the law generally treats them as supplied by the principal to the job worker on the date they were originally sent. The principal may then become liable for GST and applicable interest. Do not wait until the deadline has passed to investigate missing goods or incomplete job-work orders.

### Maintain records and claim eligible ITC

Section 19 permits the principal to take ITC on inputs or capital goods sent to a job worker, subject to the Act’s conditions. Credit may also be available where goods are sent directly by the supplier to the job worker without first being brought to the principal’s premises. The principal should retain purchase invoices, challans, job-work agreements, movement evidence and reconciliation records to support the credit and demonstrate the movement of goods.

The principal is also responsible for maintaining proper accounts of inputs and capital goods sent for job work. This makes inventory reconciliation important: quantities dispatched should be matched with quantities returned, consumed, scrapped or otherwise accounted for.

## 2. Responsibilities of the Job Worker

The job worker should receive and verify the goods against the challan, record quantities and condition, and maintain job-wise stock and processing records. If goods are sent onward to another job worker, the movement should be documented in the manner permitted by Rule 45. The challan may be issued by the principal or the job worker sending the goods onward, and the original challan may be endorsed with details of the goods moved.

The job worker generally raises a tax invoice for the job-work service supplied to the principal. Where the activity is simply processing goods belonging to the principal, GST is ordinarily charged on the job-work service value or charges, rather than on the value of the principal’s goods. The applicable rate depends on the precise service classification and relevant rate notification; there is no safe basis for assuming that every activity labelled job work attracts one universal rate.

A job worker must assess GST registration requirements based on aggregate turnover and the applicable registration provisions, including any relevant exceptions. Registration status and invoicing obligations should be reviewed rather than assumed from the size of an individual job order.

## 3. GST on Job Work Charges and ITC

For the principal, GST charged by the job worker may generally be eligible for ITC when the statutory conditions are met, the service is used in the course or furtherance of business, and the credit is not blocked or otherwise restricted. The principal should match the job worker’s invoice with the purchase order, work completion evidence and accounting records, and verify the relevant details in GST records before claiming credit.

For the job worker, the invoice should describe the processing service accurately, identify the relevant goods or work order where useful, and apply the correct classification, place-of-supply treatment and tax rate. If the job worker also supplies materials or undertakes a wider manufacturing arrangement, the transaction may require a separate classification analysis. The GST treatment should follow the actual contractual and commercial arrangement, not merely the label on the invoice.

## 4. ITC-04 Filing and Movement Documentation

Rule 45 requires the principal to report prescribed details of goods sent to a job worker, received back or moved between job workers in FORM GST ITC-04. The filing frequency depends on the principal’s aggregate turnover in the immediately preceding financial year:

- More than ₹5 crore: ITC-04 is generally filed for each six-month period—April to September and October to March—by the 25th day of the month following the relevant period.

- Up to ₹5 crore: ITC-04 is generally filed annually for the financial year, by the 25th day of the month following that financial year.

Check for any applicable extension or amendment before filing. ITC-04 should be prepared from reconciled challan data, not estimated totals. The principal should also assess e-way bill requirements for each movement. Under Rule 138, the general consignment-value threshold and the specific inter-State job-work provisions must be considered; inter-State dispatch to a job worker can trigger an e-way bill requirement irrespective of consignment value.

The CBIC’s [Circular No. 38/12/2018-GST](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/pdf/circularno-38-cgst.pdf) explains challan handling for direct dispatches, movements between job workers and returns. The [amended CGST Rules](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/pdf/amended-01012022-CGST-Rules-2017-Part-A.pdf) set out the current six-month and annual ITC-04 periods based on the ₹5 crore turnover threshold.

## A Practical Compliance Checklist for Manufacturers

- Use a numbered challan for every eligible dispatch, including direct supplier-to-job-worker movements.
- Maintain a job-worker-wise register showing dispatch dates, quantities, challan numbers and expected return dates.
- Record onward transfers, processing losses, scrap and goods returned in a traceable manner.
- Monitor the one-year input and three-year capital-goods limits under Section 143.
- Reconcile ITC-04 with challans, stock records, invoices and physical inventory.
- Review GST rates, registration status and e-way bill requirements for the actual transaction.
