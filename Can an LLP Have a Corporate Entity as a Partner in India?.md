# Can an LLP Have a Corporate Entity as a Partner in India?

Yes. A Limited Liability Partnership (LLP) in India can have a company or another eligible body corporate as a partner. The Limited Liability Partnership Act, 2008 expressly permits a body corporate to join an LLP. However, a corporate entity cannot itself act as a designated partner: an individual must be nominated to perform that role. This distinction is important for companies, holding groups and investors planning an LLP structure.

# What Does the LLP Act Say About Corporate Partners?

Section 5 of the [Limited Liability Partnership Act, 2008](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/handle/123456789/2096) states that any individual or body corporate may be a partner in an LLP, subject to the Act. The provision therefore allows an incorporated company to hold a partnership interest in an LLP. The Act also treats an LLP as a body corporate and a legal entity separate from its partners, with perpetual succession.

In practical terms, a private limited company, public limited company or another entity falling within the statutory definition of a body corporate may participate as an LLP partner. The proposed entity should still check its own constitutional documents, internal approval requirements and any sector-specific restrictions before committing funds or assets.

The minimum-partner requirement continues to apply. An LLP must have at least two partners. A corporate partner counts as one partner, but it does not remove the requirement to have the prescribed number of designated partners.

# Can a Company Become a Designated Partner?

No. A designated partner must be an individual. Section 7 requires every LLP to have at least two designated partners who are individuals. Where one or more partners are bodies corporate, the law permits individuals who are partners or nominees of the corporate partners to act as designated partners.

At least one designated partner must satisfy the statutory Indian-residency test. Under the current provision, a person is treated as resident in India for this purpose if they have stayed in India for at least 120 days during the financial year. The requirement applies to the designated partners, not to the corporate partner itself.

For example, if ABC Private Limited and an individual entrepreneur establish an LLP, ABC Private Limited can be named as a partner. It may nominate an individual to act on its behalf as a nominee or designated partner. The LLP must still ensure that it has at least two individual designated partners and that at least one meets the residency condition.

The nominated individual is not the same thing as the corporate partner. The company holds the partnership interest; the nominee is the natural person identified to undertake the designated-partner role and related statutory responsibilities.

# Documents Required When a Corporate Entity Joins an LLP

The precise document set depends on the entity type, jurisdiction and filing circumstances. For an Indian body corporate, the Ministry of Corporate Affairs’ Form FiLLiP instructions identify two central authorisations:

- A resolution on the body corporate’s letterhead approving its decision to become a partner in the proposed LLP.
- A separate resolution or authorisation, also on its letterhead, identifying the individual nominated to act as its nominee or designated partner and stating that person’s name and address.

These requirements are reflected in the MCA’s [Form FiLLiP Instruction Kit](https://www.mca.gov.in/content/dam/mca-aem-forms/instructionkits/Instruction%20Kit_Form%20FiLLiP.pdf), which also explains the information and attachments required for the incorporation webform.

Depending on the filing, the LLP may also need the corporate partner’s identification and registration details, the nominee’s identity and address information, consent and identification details for designated partners, registered-office evidence, contribution details and the proposed LLP agreement. The MCA form may require additional documents in particular cases. A valuation certificate may be needed where a non-cash contribution is proposed.

If the corporate partner is incorporated outside India, additional certification and authentication requirements can apply to documents submitted in India. Foreign investment, sectoral caps, pricing rules and reporting under applicable foreign-exchange regulations may also need review. These matters should be assessed before the entity commits to the LLP.

# How Does the Corporate Partner Participate in the LLP?

The LLP agreement should record the corporate partner’s contribution, profit-sharing ratio, decision-making rights, responsibilities, transfer restrictions, admission and retirement procedures, and dispute-resolution process.

A corporate partner may contribute money, property, intangible assets or another permitted form of contribution. The agreement should describe how it will be valued and recorded. Non-cash contributions may require additional valuation and filing steps.

# Rights, Responsibilities and Compliance Considerations

A corporate partner should review the LLP agreement before signing and ensure that its internal approvals cover the agreed contribution and commercial terms. It should also maintain records of the resolution, nominee authorisation, contribution and any later changes to its nominated representative.

Designated partners have specific compliance responsibilities under the LLP Act, including responsibility for statutory filings and compliance matters assigned to them by law and the LLP agreement. A nominee acting as a designated partner should understand these duties rather than treating the appointment as a formality.

The LLP must keep partner details current and file prescribed notices when changes occur. A change in the nominee or contribution may require supporting documents, an agreement amendment or an MCA filing. Annual and event-based filing obligations also continue.

# A Practical Checklist Before Incorporation

- Confirm that the proposed corporate partner falls within the Act’s definition of a body corporate.
- Obtain the corporate partner’s valid internal approval to join the LLP and make the proposed contribution.
- Select and formally authorise the nominee who may act as a designated partner.
- Identify at least two individual designated partners and confirm that at least one meets the Indian-residency test.
- Agree the contribution, profit share, management rights and exit provisions before finalising the LLP agreement.
- Check any foreign-investment, sectoral, licensing or regulatory conditions relevant to the proposed activity.
- Prepare the FiLLiP attachments and verify the current MCA filing requirements before submission.

# Planning the Right LLP Structure

A corporate entity can be a partner in an Indian LLP, but the arrangement should be planned around both the corporate partner’s approval process and the LLP’s statutory requirements. The most important points are to distinguish the corporate partner from its individual nominee, document the authorisations correctly, and set out commercial rights and responsibilities clearly in the LLP agreement.
