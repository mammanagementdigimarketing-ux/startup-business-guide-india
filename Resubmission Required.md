# What Happens When a Company Registration Application Is Marked for Resubmission by the MCA?

Seeing “Resubmission Required” on a company incorporation application can be worrying, especially when founders are working towards a launch date, opening a bank account or completing other business formalities. However, the status does not automatically mean that the application has been rejected. It generally means that the Registrar or Central Registration Centre has identified a defect, missing detail or point that needs clarification before the application can be processed further.

For applicants using the MCA’s SPICe+ incorporation route, the next step is to read the resubmission remarks carefully, correct the specific issue and submit the updated form and applicable linked documents within the permitted period. A careful, complete response can help the application move forward; an incomplete or late response may lead to rejection.

## What Does “Marked for Resubmission” Mean?

The Ministry of Corporate Affairs (MCA) examines incorporation forms and their attachments to check whether the information and supporting records meet the requirements of the Companies Act, 2013 and the Companies (Incorporation) Rules, 2014. If the reviewing authority needs additional information or finds a defect or omission, it may send the application back for resubmission rather than deciding it immediately.

In practical terms, an MCA application marked for resubmission is still under examination. The applicant is being asked to address the observations recorded against the relevant Service Request Number (SRN) or form. It is important to distinguish this status from a final rejection: resubmission provides an opportunity to correct the application, but approval is not guaranteed.

## Why Can an MCA Company Registration Application Be Sent Back?

The reason should be stated in the resubmission remarks. Common issues in a SPICe+ application or its linked forms include:

- **Incorrect or inconsistent details:** Names, dates of birth, residential addresses, identity details or shareholding information may differ between the form and supporting records.
- **Incomplete identity or address proof:** A document may be missing, outdated, unclear or inconsistent with the details entered in the application.
- **Registered office documentation gaps:** The application may not include the required ownership or occupancy proof, rent or lease documentation, or the property owner's no-objection certificate where applicable.
- **Problems with MOA or AOA:** The Memorandum of Association or Articles of Association may contain incomplete, inconsistent or unsuitable information, or may not have been submitted in the required manner.
- **Incorrect attachments or file quality:** Scanned copies may be illegible, a required attachment may be omitted, or a document may have been uploaded in an incorrect format.
- **Digital signature or linked-form issues:** A Digital Signature Certificate (DSC), signature process or linked form may need correction or regeneration.
- **Details requiring clarification:** The reviewing authority may ask for an explanation or further evidence about the proposed company, its subscribers, directors, registered office or objects.

The MCA’s official [FAQs on Incorporation and Allied Matters](https://www.mca.gov.in/content/dam/mca/pdf/SPICEplus-and-linked-filings-FAQs-V3-20230122.pdf) discuss examples such as missing registered-office documents, incorrect or incomplete address proof, and illegible or incorrectly submitted attachments. These examples are useful checks, but the remarks on an individual application should always guide the actual correction.

## What Is the MCA Resubmission Deadline?

Do not assume that a resubmission request can be answered at any time. The deadline is governed by the applicable rule, form process and the period stated in the MCA communication. For SPICe+ resubmission, MCA form guidance commonly requires the applicant to complete the corrected webform submission, digital-signature process and upload within 15 days from the date the application is marked for resubmission or sent back by the back-office user, as applicable.

The Companies (Incorporation) Rules, 2014 also contain provisions allowing an opportunity to remove defects and resubmit incorporation documents, including a further opportunity in specified circumstances. The precise treatment can depend on the form and the applicable rule. Therefore, applicants should rely on the deadline displayed in the MCA portal and the resubmission communication rather than treating 15 days as a universal extension for every MCA filing.

The [MCA instruction kit for Form INC-13](https://www.mca.gov.in/content/dam/mca-aem-forms/instructionkits/Instruction%20Kit_INC-13%20%28e-MoA%29.pdf), which explains the SPICe+ resubmission workflow for the relevant linked form, instructs users to access the application marked “Resubmission required”, update it, submit it, affix the DSC and upload the signed PDF. It also describes the 15-day resubmission period for the SRNs covered by that guidance.

## How to Respond to an MCA Resubmission

A structured response is usually more effective than making hurried edits. The following steps can help applicants manage the MCA resubmission process:

1. **Open the correct application:** Log in to the MCA portal using the relevant user account. Go to the application history or dashboard and locate the SPICe+ application using its SRN and current status.
2. **Read every observation:** Review all remarks, including those attached to linked forms. Note exactly what information, correction or supporting evidence is being requested. Do not rely only on the status label.
3. **Match the response to the remark:** Correct the relevant field or replace the specific document. If the remark asks for clarification, provide a concise explanation supported by the appropriate record.
4. **Check linked forms:** SPICe+ may involve linked filings such as e-MOA, e-AOA, INC-9 and AGILE-PRO-S, depending on the application. Follow the portal instructions to regenerate and resubmit the forms that are required. MCA has issued clarifications explaining that the linked forms to be resubmitted can depend on whether the remark relates to Part A, Part B or a particular linked form.
5. **Review the full application:** Before submission, compare names, addresses, dates, capital details, subscriber information and attachments across all relevant forms. Confirm that the updated files are readable and correspond to the latest information.
6. **Complete DSC and upload steps:** Submit the updated webform, complete the required digital-signature steps and upload the DSC-affixed PDF or other required documents through the MCA portal.
7. **Save proof and monitor the SRN:** Keep the resubmission acknowledgement, updated documents and SRN details. Check the portal for further remarks or a decision.

For a practical example, suppose the registered-office proof names the property owner but the application does not include the required occupancy document or owner's NOC. The applicant should not simply upload the same utility bill again. They should identify the exact document requested in the remark, obtain the correct supporting record and ensure that the address and proposed company details match throughout the submission.

## What If the Applicant Does Not Respond in Time?

If the applicant does not complete the resubmission within the allowed period, the incorporation application may be rejected or otherwise closed in accordance with the applicable process. A fresh application or a new filing may then be necessary, depending on the status of the earlier SRN, name reservation and the relevant MCA requirements. This can mean additional administrative work, revised documents and possible changes to the proposed incorporation schedule.

A rejection should not be treated as a reason to repeat the same filing without reviewing the earlier observations. First identify why the application was not accepted, whether the proposed name remains available, and which forms or documents must be prepared afresh. Where the MCA portal displays a technical issue rather than a substantive defect, use the official MCA help or grievance channel and retain the ticket reference.

## A Quick Checklist Before Resubmitting

- The SRN and resubmission remarks have been reviewed in full.
- Every observation has a corresponding correction or explanation.
- Names, dates, addresses and identification details match across forms and proofs.
- Registered-office evidence and owner consent are included where required.
- MOA, AOA and other linked forms have been checked and regenerated where instructed.
- All attachments are current, complete, legible and in the prescribed format.
- The DSC is valid and the required signing and upload steps are completed.
- The resubmission is completed before the portal-stated deadline.
- The acknowledgement and supporting records are saved for future reference.

## Reducing the Risk of Further MCA Queries

Many resubmission issues can be reduced through preparation before the first filing. Founders should confirm the proposed company name, decide the shareholding and directorship structure, verify identity and address records, and arrange registered-office documents early. The MOA and AOA should reflect the intended business and company structure, while all form entries should be checked against the supporting documents before submission.

It is also useful to assign one person to coordinate document collection and maintain a single, current set of approved records. When several founders or subscribers are involved, inconsistent versions of address proofs, names or declarations can create avoidable delays.

## Taking the Next Step with Company Incorporation

An MCA resubmission request is an opportunity to correct an application, not a confirmation that the proposed company cannot be incorporated. The most important actions are to understand the remarks, respond to each point with accurate information, complete all linked-form and DSC requirements, and meet the deadline shown on the portal.
