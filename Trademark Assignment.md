What Is Trademark Assignment in India? How Can Brand Ownership Be Transferred?

A business name or logo can become one of a company’s most valuable assets. When a business is sold, reorganised, inherited or transferred to another owner, the trademark associated with that brand may also need to change hands. Trademark assignment in India is the legal process through which ownership rights in a trademark are transferred from its current owner to another person or entity. The transfer should be documented properly and, where the mark is registered, recorded with the Trade Marks Registry so the public record reflects the new proprietor.

What Does Trademark Assignment Mean?

A trademark assignment is a transfer of proprietary rights in a trademark by its current owner (the assignor) to another person or organisation (the assignee). The mark may be a brand name, logo, label or another sign used to distinguish particular goods or services. The transfer can cover all the goods or services listed in a registration, or only a defined part of them, subject to the restrictions in the Trade Marks Act, 1999.

Sections 37 to 45 of the Trade Marks Act, 1999 deal with assignment and transmission. Section 37 recognises the registered proprietor’s power to assign the mark, while Section 38 permits assignment of a registered trademark with or without the goodwill of the business. An unregistered trademark may also be assigned under Section 39. The statutory provisions are available in the Trade Marks Act, 1999.

When Might a Business Need to Transfer Trademark Ownership?

Trademark ownership transfer may be relevant in several business situations:

•	Business sale or acquisition: the buyer may acquire the brand along with other business assets.

•	Change in business structure: a founder may transfer a personally owned mark to a company or an LLP.

•	Group restructuring: ownership may move between related entities as part of a genuine reorganisation.

•	Succession or inheritance: rights may pass to a legal successor following the owner’s death or another legally recognised event.

•	Brand portfolio transaction: a business may sell or transfer selected marks for particular product or service lines.

For example, if a founder registered a product name in their individual capacity and later operates through a private limited company, the founder and company should document whether the company is receiving ownership of the mark or merely permission to use it. A licence and an assignment are different arrangements: a licence generally permits use while ownership remains with the proprietor.

Assignment With Goodwill and Without Goodwill

Trademark Assignment With Goodwill

An assignment with goodwill transfers the trademark together with the business reputation and customer connection associated with the relevant goods or services. This is often considered when a business or a defined business division is sold. The agreement should clearly identify what goodwill and related business assets are included.

Trademark Assignment Without Goodwill

An assignment without goodwill transfers the trademark separately from the business goodwill connected with it. Indian law permits this in specified circumstances, but Section 42 imposes an additional procedure. The assignee must apply to the Registrar for directions about advertising the assignment within six months from the date of assignment, or within an extended period allowed by the Registrar that cannot exceed a further three months in aggregate, and must advertise it as directed. This requirement should be checked carefully before completing a transaction.

What Should a Trademark Transfer Agreement Include?

A written trademark assignment agreement or deed provides evidence of the transaction and helps reduce uncertainty between the parties. It should be prepared for the specific deal rather than copied from a generic template. Common points to address include:

•	Full legal names, addresses and identifying details of the assignor and assignee.

•	The trademark details, including application or registration number, representation of the mark and relevant class or classes.

•	The goods or services and the extent of rights being transferred.

•	Whether the assignment includes goodwill, and what business assets or goodwill are covered.

•	The consideration, payment terms and effective date of transfer.

•	Representations about ownership, existing licences, security interests, disputes and pending proceedings, where relevant.

•	Responsibility for filing the recordal application, responding to Registry queries and bearing applicable costs.

•	Execution requirements, including signatures, witnesses, stamping and other formalities applicable to the instrument.

The parties should also check whether the mark is subject to a licence, co-ownership arrangement, charge, court order or other third-party right. A transfer agreement cannot safely be treated as a substitute for reviewing the Registry record and the underlying commercial arrangements.

Trademark Assignment Process in India

1. Review the trademark record

Identify each trademark proposed for transfer and verify its current status on the Trade Marks Registry. Check the proprietor’s name, application or registration number, class, goods or services, renewal status, disclaimers or limitations, and any recorded licences or other interests. Also review pending objections, oppositions, cancellation proceedings or disputes that could affect the transaction.

2. Agree on the scope and prepare the deed
   
The assignor and assignee should decide whether the transfer covers the whole registration or only specified goods or services, and whether goodwill is included. The deed should describe the rights accurately and set out the commercial terms. Special restrictions may apply where an assignment could create confusingly similar exclusive rights in different owners or geographic areas; Sections 40 and 41 of the Act address these situations.

3. Execute the assignment documents

Both parties should execute the final instrument in accordance with applicable legal formalities. Keep the signed deed and supporting evidence safely. If the transfer is part of a company acquisition or restructuring, ensure that the transaction documents and corporate approvals are consistent with the trademark transfer.

4. Apply to record the transfer with IP India

Under Section 45 of the Act and Rule 75 of the Trade Marks Rules, 2017, a person who becomes entitled to a registered trademark by assignment or transmission applies to the Registrar to record their title. The prescribed request is made in Form TM-P. Rule 76 requires a duly certified copy of the original document, instrument or deed that transfers title, together with a statement of case. The Registrar may ask for further evidence or information if there is a reasonable doubt about the documents or the claim. The relevant procedure is set out in the official Trade Marks Rules, 2017. Check the current e-filing requirements and document specifications on the IP India portal before submission.

5. Respond to Registry queries and verify the entry
   
Monitor the application for examination or requests for clarification. Once the assignment is accepted and recorded, verify that the assignee’s name and the particulars of the transfer appear correctly in the Register. Retain the acknowledgement, filed documents and updated Registry record with the business’s intellectual-property records.

Documents Commonly Needed for Trademark Assignment

The exact document set depends on the transaction and the Registrar’s requirements. A practical preparation checklist may include:

•	A duly executed trademark assignment deed or agreement.

•	A certified copy of the instrument, as required under Rule 76.

•	Details or a copy of the trademark application or registration record.

•	A statement of case explaining the basis and scope of the transfer.

•	Identity and address details of the parties and, where relevant, proof of authority for the signatories.

•	Supporting corporate approvals, succession documents or other evidence of title, if relevant to the transaction.

•	Any additional evidence requested by the Registrar.

This is a general guide, not a fixed universal list. The Registry may require additional proof depending on whether the transaction is an assignment, a transmission by operation of law, a partial transfer or a transfer involving special restrictions.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

•	Signing a deed without checking that the person transferring the mark is the recorded proprietor or is otherwise legally entitled to transfer it.

•	Describing the trademark or the goods and services too vaguely, particularly in a partial assignment.

•	Failing to state clearly whether goodwill is included.

•	Overlooking Section 42 advertising requirements for an assignment without goodwill.

•	Assuming that a private agreement alone updates the public Register.

•	Ignoring licences, disputes, co-ownership or restrictions that may affect the rights being transferred.

A careful review before signing can help prevent delays, gaps in the ownership record and disagreements about what was actually transferred.

Planning a Clear and Documented Brand Transfer

Trademark assignment is not simply a change of name in a database. It is a transfer of valuable intellectual-property rights that should be supported by a properly drafted instrument, a review of the mark’s legal status and the required Registry filing. The right approach depends on the nature of the transaction, the goods or services involved and whether goodwill is transferred.
